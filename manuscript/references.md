### Notes on citation usage in the manuscript

- **Amrani et al. 2024** is the case study. Every quantitative statement attributed to
  them is taken from their published text, Table 1 (guide spacers and PAMs), Table 2
  (in-silico nominated off-target totals: ICP0g1 358, ICP0g2 910, ICP27g1 443,
  ICP27g2 316), Table 3 (no sites at ≤ 3 total mismatches plus bulges for either lead
  guide), Figure S6 (0.78–1.65% mean indel frequency in trigeminal ganglia at day 40),
  and their Methods (ViPR February 2022 snapshot; 9,409 NCBI Nucleotide IDs; the
  `>70%` criterion; BWA `aln` + Cas-OFFinder with PAM `NNGRRN`; GUIDE-seq validation).
  Everything else attributed to their guides in this manuscript is **our own
  measurement of their published sequences**, not a quotation of their numbers, and is
  labelled as such in the text.
- **Bae et al. 2014** supports only the identification of Cas-OFFinder as the
  field-standard PAM-aware off-target search tool and the tool Amrani et al. used.
  It does **not** support the bulge claim: the 2014 paper describes mismatch counting
  and PAM variation only, and contains no mention of DNA or RNA bulges. The bulge
  search is a later addition to the tool. The manuscript now says exactly that.
- **Chen et al. 2019** and **van Overbeek et al. 2016** support only the statement that
  large-scale empirical profiles of Cas9 repair outcomes exist and are reproducible but
  strongly target-sequence dependent, so that no single indel-length spectrum is a
  correct universal default. Neither is the source of the spectrum used in our model,
  which remains a labelled assumption.
- **Clopper and Pearson 1934** supports the exact binomial confidence interval used for
  the resolution floor. **Hanley and Lippman-Hand 1983** supports the rule-of-three
  approximation quoted alongside it (3/*n*). The manuscript now distinguishes the two:
  0.0162 is the exact Clopper–Pearson limit, 0.0164 the rule-of-three approximation.
- **Cohen 2020** supports only the statement that herpesviruses, including HSV-1,
  establish lifelong latency in which the viral genome persists in sensory neurons and
  is not cleared by available therapy. **James et al. 2020** supports only the
  epidemiological framing (global HSV-1 prevalence and incidence).
- **Doench et al. 2016** and **Hsu et al. 2013** support only the statement that the
  MIT/Hsu mismatch-position weights, CFD and Rule Set 2 activity models were fitted to
  SpCas9 data and are therefore not parameterised for SaCas9.
- **Gao et al. 2018** supports the poly-T filter. Note the precise finding: T₄ is the
  *minimal* type-3 Pol III termination signal and termination becomes efficient only at
  T₆ or longer. The manuscript now states this rather than the flat "terminates at
  ≥ 4 T", and frames the `TTTT` exclusion as a conservative design choice.
- **Lin et al. 2014** supports only the statement that Cas9 has measurable off-target
  activity at sites carrying a DNA or RNA bulge, i.e. that a bulge-tolerant search is
  addressing a real phenomenon.
- **Metsky et al. 2022** supports only the statement that per-sequence presence
  matrices and joint (rather than marginal) coverage optimisation are established
  practice in the viral-diagnostic design literature.
- **Ondov et al. 2016** supports the MinHash sketching and mash distance/ANI estimator
  used in the effective-sample-size analysis.
- **Ramadoss et al. 2025** supports the repair-pathway statements about the target cell
  type: that resting human neurons resolve Cas9-induced breaks over a longer window
  than isogenic dividing cells, upregulate non-canonical repair factors, and favour
  NHEJ over MMEJ, yielding a narrower distribution of smaller indels. It is used in
  Limitation 5 only, and does **not** supply a spectrum for the model.
- **Ran et al. 2015** supports the SaCas9 PAM statement. Read it carefully: it reports
  `NNGRRT` as the PAM at which SaCas9 cleaves *most efficiently*, and simultaneously
  reports sixth-position degeneracy, concluding that all `NNGRR` PAMs can be cleaved
  and should be considered as potential targets. The manuscript's priority of the
  canonical tier over the permissive tier is therefore a stated weighting, not a claim
  that `NNGRRN` sites are inert, and Section 2.5 now says so.
- **Sfeir and Symington 2015** supports only the statement that MMEJ produces a
  distinct, microhomology-driven deletion spectrum and that its usage is pathway- and
  context-dependent.
- **Tsai et al. 2015** supports only the description of GUIDE-seq as a cell-based,
  genome-wide off-target detection method — the class of evidence this manuscript
  does not contain and Amrani et al. performed.
- **Wang et al. 2016** supports only the specific statement that CRISPR-induced NHEJ
  repair products at a target site can accelerate the emergence of escape variants in
  a chronic viral infection. It is not used to support any HSV-1-specific claim.
- **Wei et al. 2023** and **Yin et al. 2021** support only the statement that CRISPR
  anti-HSV programs have reached animals and, in one case, patients. **Note the author
  correction:** these were carried in working notes as "Cai/Hong et al."; Cai Y and
  Hong J are the senior/corresponding authors, but the correct first authors are
  **Yin D** (2021) and **Wei A** (2023). The volume and page ranges in the working
  notes were correct.
- **Yates et al. 2026** ("Ensembl 2026") is the current Ensembl resource paper and
  supports the use of the Ensembl GRCh38 primary assembly and GTF. The specific
  release used (116) is pinned in Methods 5.8 by file name, size and SHA-256 rather
  than by citation.

9. **Empirical NHEJ indel-length spectrum.** **RESOLVED IN PART; THE ASSUMPTION

## Deliberately un-cited claims

The following statements are made without citation **on purpose**, because they are
measurements reported in this manuscript rather than claims about prior literature.
They are listed here so a reviewer can confirm the omission is intentional:

- Every conservation, joint-conservation, escape-probability and off-target figure
  attributed to a guide, including all figures attributed to the four published
  Amrani et al. guides, which are **our measurements of their sequences**.
- The 0.840 local 200 bp GC content around the ICP0g2 site (our measurement; it
  independently corroborates their reported ~85%, which is cited to them).
- The recurrent G→A substitution at ICP0g2 spacer position 9, its 25 carrier genomes,
  the 0 ambiguity gaps and 3 unresolved records, and the 13 independent 99.9%-identity
  clusters — all measured here from the 183-genome corpus and reported for the first
  time in this manuscript.
- The wall-clock and algorithmic performance figures for both scanners.
- **The NHEJ indel-length spectrum used in the escape model's repair term.** This is an
  explicitly labelled assumption with a documented override and a sensitivity sweep,
  not an uncited fact. Limitation 5 now says why no published spectrum was adopted in
  its place, with citations supporting that reasoning. It is listed here so that the
  absence of an attribution is unambiguously read as deliberate.
